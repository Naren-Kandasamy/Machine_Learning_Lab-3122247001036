\section{Objective}
To systematically analyze the empirical effect of dimensionality reduction using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) at 95\% explained variance retention compared to the original feature space (No-PCA) across \textbf{all 6 primary classification datasets} utilized in prior laboratory experiments (Ex 1 to 7). For each dataset under both settings, 10 distinct classifiers are evaluated under 5-fold stratified cross-validation within a leakage-free pipeline, followed by a focused 3-tier statistical significance testing suite.

\section{Problem Statement}
High-dimensional datasets frequently suffer from multicollinearity, redundant feature noise, computational overhead, and the curse of dimensionality. The objective is to evaluate whether projecting features onto orthogonal principal component directions capturing $\ge 95\%$ cumulative variance preserves predictive performance, and to rigorously test whether observed metric variations across models and cross-validation folds are statistically significant ($p < 0.05$) or merely stochastic variance.

\section{Data Preprocessing}
Standardization is a mandatory prerequisite for PCA to prevent high-magnitude features from dominating eigenvalue decomposition:
$$\mathbf{\mu} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{x}_i, \quad \mathbf{\sigma} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{\mu})^2}, \quad \mathbf{X}_{\text{scaled}} = \frac{\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{\mu}}{\mathbf{\sigma}}$$
To guarantee zero data leakage during 5-fold cross-validation, standardization and PCA transformations are placed inside a `Pipeline([('scaler', StandardScaler()), ('pca', PCA(n\_components=k)), ('clf', model)])`. In every fold, transformations are fit strictly on the training partition and evaluated out-of-sample on the validation partition.

\section{Machine Learning Algorithm}
\subsection{Principal Component Analysis (PCA)}
PCA computes the sample covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma} = \frac{1}{N-1} \mathbf{X}_{\text{scaled}}^T \mathbf{X}_{\text{scaled}}$ and solves the characteristic equation:
$$\mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{v}_i = \lambda_i \mathbf{v}_i, \quad \text{where } \lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_D \ge 0$$
The minimal number of components $d$ is selected by enforcing:
$$\frac{\sum_{i=1}^d \lambda_i}{\sum_{j=1}^D \lambda_j} \ge 0.95$$

\subsection{Evaluated Classifiers (10 Models)}
We evaluate 10 standard algorithms: Support Vector Machine (RBF kernel), Gaussian Naive Bayes, $k$-Nearest Neighbors ($k=5$), Logistic Regression (L2 penalty), Decision Tree (CART), Random Forest (100 trees), AdaBoost (50 stumps), Gradient Boosting (100 estimators), XGBoost (100 estimators), and Stacking Classifier (Base: LR, RF, KNN; Meta: Logistic Regression).

\subsection{Statistical Significance Testing Framework}
In accordance with \textbf{Unit V Course Notes (pages 11--39)}, we avoid arbitrary testing and implement \textbf{only the 3 mathematically apt and necessary tests}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Paired Two-Sample $t$-Test on CV Folds):} Matched pairs testing on identical validation folds:
    $$t = \frac{\bar{d}}{s_d / \sqrt{5}}, \quad \text{df}=4$$
    Preceded by the Shapiro-Wilk normality test on fold differences $\Delta_i$. If normality fails ($p < 0.05$), the Wilcoxon test is used as fallback.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test Across Models):} Tests whether PCA significantly shifts median model performance across heterogeneous model families on a dataset ($n=10$) and globally ($n=60$):
    $$W = \sum_{i=1}^n \text{sgn}(d_i) \cdot \text{Rank}(|d_i|)$$
    \item \textbf{Tier 3 (Friedman Omnibus Rank Test):} Non-parametric two-way ANOVA by ranks evaluating whether algorithm performance rankings differ significantly across benchmark blocks:
    $$\chi_F^2 = \frac{12 N}{k(k+1)} \left[ \sum_{j=1}^k R_j^2 - \frac{k(k+1)^2}{4} \right]$$
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Excluded Tests:} Independent $t$-test and One-Way ANOVA are excluded because models are evaluated on identical folds (repeated measures). McNemar's test is excluded as it applies to single-split binary prediction tables rather than 5-fold cross-validated continuous distributions.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Answers to Faculty Observation Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Which models improved most with PCA? Which did not? Why?} \\
    Linear and maximum-margin models (Logistic Regression, SVM) improved most or maintained top performance. In the Friedman analysis, Logistic Regression improved from rank 4.42 to 3.17, and SVM tied for the top rank (3.17). PCA maximizes global variance along orthogonal eigenvectors, eliminating multicollinearity and stabilizing linear hyperplanes. Conversely, Decision Trees degraded severely (rank 8.25 $\to$ 9.67, $\Delta F1 = -0.2007$ on Loan Approval) because axis-aligned decision trees partition one coordinate at a time; rotating features into dense linear combinations destroys natural semantic thresholds.
    \item \textbf{Did PCA reduce variance across folds (more stable results)?} \\
    Yes, for high-dimensional continuous data. On Breast Cancer, cross-validation standard deviation fell for SVM (0.0160 $\to$ 0.0119) and Logistic Regression (0.0106 $\to$ 0.0058) due to noise removal. On low-dimensional data (Iris), compressing 4 features to 2 increased Decision Tree variance (0.0171 $\to$ 0.0533) due to loss of critical boundary variance.
    \item \textbf{For high-dimensional data, was PCA beneficial in reducing overfitting?} \\
    Yes. On Breast Cancer (30 features, 569 samples), PCA reduced dimension by 66.7\% with zero performance degradation ($F1 = 0.9719$). On Optical Digits (64 features, 1,797 samples), reducing to 40 components yielded statistically indistinguishable performance ($W=11.0, p=0.1055 > 0.05$), proving that high-frequency noise was removed without damaging manifold structure.
    \item \textbf{How did linear models behave compared to ensemble models with PCA?} \\
    Linear models showed superior resilience and gained relative rank. Ensemble models (AdaBoost, XGBoost) suffered significant degradation on sparse word/tabular features (Spambase $p=0.0137$, Loan Approval $p=0.0059$) because orthogonal rotation smears isolated indicator signals across all dimensions.
    \item \textbf{Did stacking show robustness to dimensionality reduction compared to single models?} \\
    Yes. Stacking achieved rank 3.17 (No-PCA) and rank 3.33 (With-PCA), ranking among the top two models across all datasets. Blending diverse model families (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, KNN) effectively buffered against individual model degradation under compressed representations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
Experiment 7 successfully benchmarked PCA dimensionality reduction across 6 datasets and 10 classifiers. Statistical significance testing verified that PCA is highly effective for high-dimensional continuous and collinear data, providing 37.5\%--66.7\% compression without significant metric loss. Furthermore, the Friedman omnibus test established that algorithm choice under PCA is statistically significant ($\chi_F^2 = 26.75, p = 0.0015$), with linear models and Stacking outperforming axis-aligned tree architectures on compressed feature spaces.
